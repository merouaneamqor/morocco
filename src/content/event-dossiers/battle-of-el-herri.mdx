> The best-documented Moroccan military victory over France, and the one where the French record is most detailed — because the French army investigated its own catastrophe.

## Identification

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date | **13 November 1914** |
| Place | El Herri, c. 15 km from Khénifra · approx. 32°51′27″N 05°37′21″W |
| Context | Zaian War (1914–1921); three months into the First World War |
| Outcome | Decisive Zaian victory |

## Forces

**French column** — Lieutenant-Colonel **René Laverdure**, commanding at Khénifra.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Strength | **43 officers and 1,187 men** — roughly half what Laverdure had commanded in September |
| Infantry | Six companies of Algerian and Senegalese tirailleurs; a French colonial infantry detachment |
| Cavalry | A squadron of Tunisian spahis |
| Artillery | Two batteries — 65 mm mountain guns and 75 mm field guns |
| Irregulars | Goumiers |

**Zaian forces** — **Mouha ou Hammou Zayani**, with elements of Mrabtin, Aït Harkat, Aït Ischak and Aït Ichkern.

**Strength: c. 5,000 assembled during the French withdrawal.** This is a **French estimate**, made under fire by a force being destroyed, and there is no independent verification. It also describes the *peak* of a force that accumulated during the day — the number present at the opening raid was far smaller.

## Sequence

**02:30** — Laverdure marches from Khénifra **without informing his superiors**, leaving a note stating he would annihilate Hammou's camp.

**Dawn** — Complete surprise. The column finds roughly 100 tents with most fighting men absent. Artillery bombardment, then cavalry charges clear the encampment. Tribesmen rally on a hill to the south; infantry clear it. **Two of Mouha ou Hammou's wives are taken. He escapes.**

**c. 08:30** — The French loot the camp and begin to withdraw.

**Morning** — Harassment by small parties. Then the decisive fact: **the Zaian realise how small the column is.** Word spreads; a force estimated at 5,000 assembles.

**Withdrawal** — Pressure on flanks and rear; high ground occupied for aimed fire. French artillery covers the retreat but loses effectiveness against dispersed targets in broken terrain.

**The collapse** — Laverdure detaches one company of Senegalese infantry to escort the wounded to Khénifra. Other troops see it leaving and break ranks to follow. At the crossing of the **Chbouka** river the rearguard and the artillery are surrounded and overrun; the gun crews are killed.

**The end** — The survivors form square on open ground under fire from the surrounding ridges. It does not last long. Men who ran were hunted down.

**Around noon** — The wounded, their escort of 171, and **426 able-bodied survivors** reach Khénifra just ahead of the pursuit.

## Casualties

**French — well documented, from French returns:**

| Category | Killed |
|---|---|
| Algerian / Tunisian tirailleurs | 218 |
| French soldiers | 210 |
| French officers | 33 |
| Senegalese tirailleurs | 125 |
| Moroccan goumiers | 37 |
| **Total killed** | **623** |
| Wounded | 176 (171 other ranks, 5 officers) |

Roughly **65% of the column killed or wounded**. Officer losses around 90% killed or wounded — only four officers came out unhurt, all cavalry.

**Material lost:** 4 machine guns, 630 small arms, 62 horses, 56 mules, **all the artillery**, camping equipment and personal effects.

**Zaian — "at least 182 killed".**

**This figure must be handled with care.** It is a battlefield count made by the losing side, of dead left on ground the victors held. It is almost certainly a floor, not a total. The 1991 Moroccan monument at El Herri also gives 182 — which means the Moroccan commemorative figure derives from the French count rather than corroborating it.

> **Evidence note.** The monument is not a source. The 1991 obelisk lists 182 Moroccan dead, states **700** French dead (against the French return of 623), and dates the battle to **1912**. Two of its three figures are wrong. Commemoration and documentation are different things and this is a clean illustration of the difference.

## Consequences

**Immediate.** Khénifra invested. Henrys sent relief — Colonel **Garnier-Duplessix**'s *groupe mobile* arrived 16 November, Henrys himself on 18 November. By month's end the garrison stood at c. 7,000, a record. Show-of-force expeditions on 19–20 November.

**The exchange.** Mouha ou Hammou took Laverdure's body and those of six officers as trophies, and later **exchanged them for his two captured wives**. A negotiated exchange between belligerents — not the behaviour of the <ColonialTerm term="bandit" display="bandits" /> of French administrative vocabulary, and a detail worth keeping for exactly that reason.

**Political.** Hammou displayed the captured weapons to neighbouring tribes; recruitment rose sharply. The demonstration that France could be beaten was worth more than the four machine guns.

**Strategic.** Henrys abandoned negotiation for *"submit or starve"* — blockade, crop denial and economic strangulation rather than pursuit. By 1917 a military road through the Middle Atlas constrained Zaian movement. The war ground on for seven more years.

**Metropolitan.** The disaster influenced French thinking about how many troops Morocco could safely release to the Western Front. The Zaian war is therefore part of the history of the First World War, not a colonial footnote to it.

## Unresolved questions

1. **Actual Zaian losses.** Probably unrecoverable. Any Amazigh oral-tradition material bearing on it would be the only possible counter-source.
2. **Laverdure's motive.** Ambition is the French verdict, delivered about a man who could not answer. Was there intelligence — a report that Hammou was personally in the camp — that made the raid look justified? The Khénifra intelligence file for early November 1914 would show it.
3. **The command chain.** How much did Henrys know of Laverdure's intentions? The correspondence between Khénifra and the regional command in the preceding fortnight is the test.
4. **Zaian decision-making.** Was the counter-attack directed by Hammou or an accumulation of local initiative? The French account implies emergent mass action. No Zaian source has been used.
