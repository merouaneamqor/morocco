> **Standing rule: where sources disagree, the disagreement is the finding. Do not silently choose.**

## How each archive lies

Not "is biased" — *lies*, in the specific and predictable ways an institution's paperwork lies about the thing it was built to do.

### French Protectorate sources

**Systematically reliable on:** their own casualties, unit strengths, dates, movements, logistics. Armies count their own dead accurately because pensions and replacements depend on it.

**Systematically unreliable on:**

- **Enemy numbers.** Inflated when explaining defeat, deflated when claiming victory. The "c. 5,000 Zaian" at El Herri was estimated by men being destroyed.
- **Moroccan political motives.** Resistance is filed as fanaticism, banditry, feudal coercion or foreign manipulation — never as politics. A category error built into the filing system.
- **The legitimacy of Moroccan authority.** *Dissident* and *insoumis* mean "outside French authority." They are reproduced constantly as if they meant "illegitimate."
- **Civilian effects.** Blockade, crop denial and the "submit or starve" policy generate almost no documentation of their human cost, because no French institution was tasked with recording it.

**The deepest structural distortion:** the Protectorate's legitimating claim was that it *preserved* Moroccan sovereignty. Every document is written inside that fiction. It is not incidental — it is the frame.

### Spanish Protectorate sources

**Reliable on:** the internal Spanish political fight, because that fight was conducted through documents — the **Expediente Picasso** exists because Spaniards wanted to blame each other.

**Unreliable on:**

- **Their own casualties**, uniquely. The range for Annual runs 7,875 to 13,363 and it is a *Spanish* range, driven by whether Moroccan troops in Spanish service are counted.
- **Chemical weapons.** Documented in authorisation and supply; systematically undocumented in effect. No Spanish record counts who died of gas.
- **Moroccan casualties.** Essentially absent.

**Structural distortion:** Spanish sources are overwhelmingly about *Spain*. Morocco is the setting for a Spanish argument about the army, the monarchy and the nation. Moroccans appear as a problem, rarely as agents.

### Moroccan nationalist sources

**Reliable on:** the urban nationalist movement's own organisation, its internal debates, its texts.

**Unreliable on:**

- **The relationship between rural resistance and urban nationalism.** These were largely separate movements. The national narrative retrospectively unifies them.
- **Collaboration.** Personalised in **El Glaoui**, which spares the several hundred other pashas, qaids and notables who made the system work.
- **Post-1956 continuity.** The colonial security apparatus was inherited, not dismantled — **Oufkir** is the proof. This is not comfortable and is not emphasised.
- **Casualty and participation figures** in resistance episodes, which are commemorative rather than documentary. **The El Herri monument gets two of its three figures wrong.**

### Amazigh sources and oral tradition

**Reliable on:** what the affected communities remember, transmitted through *izlan* and *timdyazin* — which for the rural conquest is often the *only* surviving account from the Moroccan side.

**Unreliable on:** chronology, which oral form compresses and reorders; and numbers, which perform emphasis rather than count.

**Rule:** neither automatically true nor automatically false. Oral tradition is strong evidence of experience and weak evidence of dates. Treat it exactly as one would treat a memoir.

### British and American sources

**Value:** genuinely third-party for most of the period, and therefore the best available check on Franco-Spanish claims. FO 371 and State Department files are systematically underused in Moroccan historiography.

**Caution:** Britain had its own Moroccan policy until 1904 and its own Mediterranean interests after. Walter Harris, the most quoted British source, was a journalist writing for effect.

### German sources

**Value:** Germany had an interest in embarrassing France and Spain, which makes German reporting a useful counterweight — German estimates of Casablanca 1907 deaths run three to five times French ones. Germany was also a **participant**: it financed and armed Moroccan resistance in the First World War, and it supplied Spain's chemical weapons capability through **Hugo Stoltzenberg**.

**Caution:** for exactly the same reason. And note that after 1914 Germany is no longer a neutral observer of Morocco but a belligerent operating inside it.

### Ottoman sources — added Session 2

**Value:** the Ottoman Empire ran its own operation in Morocco from 1914 through the Teşkilât-ı Mahsusa, based in Madrid. Its officers reported to Istanbul, and they reported **inconvenient things about their German allies** — which makes the Ottoman file an unusually candid source on the whole wartime subversion effort.

**Two findings this workspace could not have obtained elsewhere:** Moulay Abdelhafid's Barcelona negotiation of 1915, and Taher Bey's observation that a large share of the notables Ottoman intelligence decorated as resistance figures were prospering under the French.

**Caution:** Ottoman reporting inflates the prospects of pan-Islamic mobilisation, because that was the premise of the operation and of the officers' own careers. Their strength figures for allied Moroccan forces should be treated exactly as French estimates of enemy strength are treated.

**Uniquely valuable feature:** these are the **only document-level archival references currently in this workspace** — real BOA strings from published scholarship, usable on a call slip.

## Method rules that follow

1. **Never state a single casualty figure where a range exists.** Give the range and the definitions.
2. **Never reproduce a colonial administrative label as description.** Quote it, attribute it, explain what the filing category meant.
3. **A commemorative monument is not a source.**
4. **When both sides benefit from a story, check it.** The fratricide at Azlag n'Tazemmourt is the type-case.
5. **Distinguish *unknown* from *withheld*.** Silvestre's death is unknown. Ben Barka's file is withheld. These require different language and different follow-up.
6. **Where a European report describes a Moroccan's motives, treat it as evidence about the reporter.**
7. **Never merge people with similar names.** See the open entry on "Sidi Mohamed Ben Hammou".
