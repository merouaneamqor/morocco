> **The purpose of going global is not coverage. It is triangulation.** A dispute between France and Spain cannot be settled by France or Spain. This page maps every live dispute in the workspace to the archive of a state that had no stake in that particular quarrel.

## The principle

Moroccan history has been written overwhelmingly from the records of the two states that conquered it. Those states disagreed with each other constantly, which historians have sometimes mistaken for a form of balance. It is not. **Two interested parties disagreeing is not corroboration.**

The useful question for any claim is: *which state was watching, kept records, and had no reason to lie about this particular thing?*

| Period | Who was watching without a stake | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1830–1904 | **Britain** | Wanted a viable Morocco as a check on France; reported accurately because it needed accurate information |
| 1904–1914 | **Germany**, and Britain residually | Germany's interest was in embarrassing France, so it over-reported French abuses — but it reported them |
| 1914–1918 | **Ottoman Empire** | Running its own operation; its officers reported what they saw to Istanbul, including inconvenient things about their German allies |
| 1918–1939 | **Britain** | No claim in Morocco; watched Spain closely because of Gibraltar |
| 1940–1956 | **United States** | Not a colonial power in Morocco, courted by both the palace and the Istiqlal |
| 1956–1999 | **United States, France** | Both have documents; both are still withholding some |

## What the global pass actually changed

Two entries moved. Both moved because a **third state's archive** was consulted.

### 1. Moulay Abdelhafid was not politically inert in exile

**Previously:** a pensioned ex-sultan, finished after 1912; German attempts to use him as a figurehead noted vaguely and dismissed.

**Now, on Ottoman records:** in **January–February 1915 he was in Barcelona negotiating with the Ottoman officer Taher Bey** the terms on which he would return to lead a general rising against France. He demanded international recognition, **300,000 marks**, and — if it failed — *a palace in Istanbul and a salary suitable for his position*. **Enver Pasha approved support in February 1915.**

**Why neither France nor Morocco recorded this prominently.** France had no interest in publicising that the sultan who signed the Protectorate was trying to overthrow it. Moroccan nationalist historiography had no use for a would-be liberator demanding a palace as his downside protection. **It took a third archive, in a third language, held by a state that no longer exists.**

References cited by Kaya and Moreau: **BOA HR.SYS. D.2392 G.5** (the Barcelona meeting); **D.2109 G.10** (Enver's approval).

### 2. Roosevelt did make promises at Anfa — and no transcript exists, which FRUS itself says

**Session 2, on Stenner (2014):** Roosevelt made supportive statements; the US government subsequently denied them; Moroccan nationalists built the denial and the promise together into a political instrument. "Unknown" was the wrong classification.

**Session 3 — verified directly.** **FRUS, Casablanca 1943, Document 376** was consulted. It is an **editorial note, not a memorandum of conversation**: the American official series states in effect that **no single official record of the dinner exists**, and reconstructs it from Hopkins's notes, McCrea's sketch, Roosevelt's press conference, and the memoirs of Elliott Roosevelt, Pendar and Murphy.

It establishes the attendance (the Sultan, the Grand Vizier, the Chief of Protocol, the Crown Prince, Churchill, **Noguès**, Patton), the topics (post-war economic development, American participation, *"the colonial question as it applied to Morocco"*), and that Roosevelt expressed sympathy with Morocco's aspirations for independence.

**And it records that Noguès had difficulty hearing the President's private conversation with the Sultan.** The Resident-General was at the table and could not hear what was said to the man he governed. Not a transcript — but contemporaneous evidence that the conversation happened, out of French earshot, which is the point both the French denial and the nationalist claim have always turned on.

**Final status:** the substance is Established; the wording is **permanently Unknown**, and the site should say so rather than leave the question open. `Consulted directly` — the first primary source in this workspace read at document level rather than through a scholar.

## The uncomfortable finding

One piece of Ottoman evidence deserves separating out, because it cuts across every national narrative in this workspace.

**Taher Bey compiled a list of Moroccan notables to receive Ottoman and German decorations in April 1915.** A scholar working from the file notes that *"a remarkable part consisted of people…who achieved important positions in full harmony with the French."*

Ottoman intelligence was decorating, as resistance figures, men who were simultaneously prospering inside the Protectorate.

**This is contemporaneous, third-party, documentary evidence that for a large part of the Moroccan notability, resistance and collaboration were not alternatives but a portfolio.** It is inconvenient to the French account (which needed clean categories to administer), to the nationalist account (which needed clean categories to build a nation), and to the Amazigh account (which needs them to argue exclusion). It survives because the people who wrote it down were arguing about something else entirely.

**That is the whole case for going global.**
